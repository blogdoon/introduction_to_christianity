01 — The map

The Creed is the table of contents

Ratzinger walks through the whole faith by walking through the Apostles' Creed. So the Creed is your progress bar: every article is a cluster of ideas. Click an article to open its deep-dive and the ideas behind it.

    02 — The ideas

    One big idea per chapter

    Each card holds the single sentence worth keeping, the question to carry into the reading, Ratzinger's twist, the misreading he fights, how he argues, and his own words. Tap a card to open it.

      04 — The recall

      Practice: fill the blank

      Sentences straight from Ratzinger, one key phrase removed. Retrieval — not rereading — is what makes a memory permanent.

      05 — The method

      How Ratzinger argues: the art of both/and

      Nearly every chapter performs the same move: two camps, each half-right, and the Creed's third position holding both. Learn the move and you can read anything he wrote. Each idea card carries its own argument map.

      06 — The architecture

      Five distinctions that hold the book up

      Ratzinger argues through pairs. Learn the pairs and the whole book snaps into place. Click one side to see the ideas that turn on it.

      07 — The vocabulary

      Terms that carry the nuance

      Ratzinger's precision lives in borrowed words — Latin, Greek, Hebrew, German. Learn these and the fine distinctions stop slipping away.

      08 — The conversation

      The thinkers he argues with

      The book is quietly positioned in a great dialogue — allies whose language he borrows, foils whose claims he answers. Know the conversation and the book's every sentence snaps into focus.

      09 — The sources

      His own corrections & his Scripture

      The prefaces are Ratzinger's errata on his own book; the Bible is the ground he builds on. Both are the shortest route to the nuances he most wanted understood.
